title: Simple Sentences
icon: 📝
summary: Subject, verb and object; I am, you are, he is.

[basics]
1. A simple sentence has a subject (who or what) and a verb (the action or state): "Birds fly."
2. Many sentences add an object, which receives the action: subject + verb + object. "I eat rice." "She reads a book."
3. The verb "to be" has three present forms: I am, you are, he/she/it is. "I am happy." "You are kind." "She is a teacher."
4. For we, you and they, use "are": "We are friends." "They are at home."
5. Start with a capital letter and end with a full stop: "The cat is small."
6. To say no, add "not": "I am not tired." "He is not here."

[expert]
- With he, she and it, most verbs add -s in the present: "I like tea" but "she likes tea".
- Word order matters in English. "The dog bites the man" and "The man bites the dog" mean different things.
- Short forms are common in speech: I'm, you're, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're, isn't, aren't.
- Use "a" for one thing, and no "a" for more than one: "a dog", "two dogs".
- Add more detail slowly: "I eat rice", then "I eat rice at home", then "I eat rice at home every day".
