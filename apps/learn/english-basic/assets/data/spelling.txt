title: Spelling Tips
icon: ✏️
summary: Silent e, double letters, plurals and common mix-ups.

[basics]
1. Silent e: a final e is not said, but it makes the vowel say its name. "cap" becomes "cape", "kit" becomes "kite".
2. Double letters: some short words double the last letter before -ing or -ed. "run" becomes "running", "stop" becomes "stopped".
3. Plurals: for most words add -s: "book" becomes "books". After s, x, ch or sh add -es: "box" becomes "boxes", "dish" becomes "dishes".
4. There, their, they're: "there" is a place ("Sit there."), "their" shows ownership ("their house"), "they're" means "they are".
5. To, too, two: "to" shows direction ("go to school"), "too" means also or very ("me too", "too hot"), "two" is the number 2.
6. Check new words by looking at them, covering them, writing them, and then checking again.

[expert]
- Some plurals are irregular: man becomes men, child becomes children, foot becomes feet, and sheep stays sheep.
- When a word ends in consonant + y, change y to ies: "baby" becomes "babies", "city" becomes "cities".
- "i before e, except after c" helps with words like "believe" and "receive", but there are exceptions such as "weird".
- "Your" shows ownership ("your bag") and "you're" means "you are". Say the long form to check which one fits.
- Reading a lot is the best way to improve spelling, because you see correct spelling many times.
