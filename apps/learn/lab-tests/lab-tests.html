    📘 How to Read Your Lab Report

    1. Reference Intervals
    Every laboratory establishes a "reference interval" based on 95% of healthy individuals tested in that laboratory. Because equipment, reagents, and calibration vary between diagnostic centers, always check the specific reference column on your personal slip.
    2. Out-of-Range Flags (H, L, Asterisks)
    Laboratories flag numbers outside their mathematical reference range with an "H" (High), "L" (Low), or an asterisk (*). Being slightly outside does not automatically mean a disease; benign fluctuations occur with hydration, stress, recent meals, or vigorous exercise.
    3. Fasting Requirements
    Tests like Fasting Blood Glucose, Lipid Profiles (Triglycerides), and certain metabolic panels require an 8–12 hour fast. Drinking plain water is usually encouraged to stay hydrated, as dehydration artificially concentrates blood cells and proteins.

    💡 Common Lab Panels Demystified

    CBC (Complete Blood Count)
    Counts red blood cells, hemoglobin (oxygen), white blood cells (immunity), and platelets (clotting). Gives an immediate overview of infection, inflammation, or anemia.
    KFT / RFT (Kidney Function Tests)
    Measures Creatinine, Blood Urea, and Electrolytes (Sodium, Potassium). Tells your doctor how efficiently the kidneys filter metabolic waste from the bloodstream.
    LFT (Liver Function Tests)
    Evaluates liver cellular health through enzymes (SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST, ALP) and bile processing (Total and Direct Bilirubin, Albumin).
