How to use

  1. Add foods. Search or pick a food group, then tap a food to add one portion, or drag it onto the plate. Use + and − to change the amount.
  2. Portions are everyday measures: 1 roti, 1 katori, 1 cup or 1 piece. Nutrient figures are rounded guides from Indian food tables (IFCT 2017), not a laboratory test.
  3. The balanced-plate guide uses food weight, so vegetables stay visible: about half vegetables and fruit, one quarter grains, and one quarter protein (dal, sprouts, eggs, fish or meat), with milk or curd on the side. Oils, snacks and sweets should stay small.
  4. Plan a day. Switch between breakfast, lunch, snacks and dinner. Set age, sex, activity and diet for a rough daily target. Bars and short tips compare the whole day with that target.
  5. Sex and targets. Targets follow ICMR-NIN style figures and stay approximate. Iron is higher for most females from the pre-teen years to adulthood. Prefer not to say uses the mid-point of the female and male figures. Older adults use the same iron figure for both.
  6. Games. Guess the calories of a food, fix an unbalanced plate by adding and removing foods, or pick a healthier swap. Best scores stay on this device.
  7. Save. Named plates and days are stored in this browser only.
  8. More foods (NP2 and NP3). A Leafy greens group (lai xaak, khutora, sojina paat, kolmou, morapat, kochu xaak, dhekia and more) and a Condiments group (ginger, garlic, chillies, spices, nemu, tamarind, outenga, pickle, kharisa and mustard oil). Vegetables now include tomato, onion, carrot, gourds such as potol and komora, tubers such as kochu and ol, and garden foods such as koldil, posola and sojina. Grains add Basmati, Joha, bora saul and red rice. Meat and fish add pork, dry fish (hukoti, namsing / sidol), plain broiler and masor tenga. Search works with local names. Assam foods with no exact table row use the closest IFCT 2017 food, so they stay approximate. Leafy greens count as vegetables on the plate; condiments sit on the side.
