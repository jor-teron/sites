How to use

  1. Add foods. Search or pick a food group, then tap a food to add one of your portions, or drag it onto the plate. Use + and − to change how many portions are on the plate. Type grams on a plate item for that serving only — this does not change your usual size.
  2. Portions (NP4). Defaults are everyday measures: 1 roti, 1 katori, 1 cup or 1 piece. Open the Portions tab to set your usual size in grams; leave a box blank to keep the default. Reset one food or Reset all to go back to the defaults. Nutrient figures scale from per-gram values in Indian food tables (IFCT 2017) and stay rounded guides, not a laboratory test.
  3. The balanced-plate guide uses food weight, so vegetables stay visible: about half vegetables and fruit, one quarter grains, and one quarter protein (dal, sprouts, eggs, fish or meat), with milk or curd on the side. Oils, snacks and sweets should stay small.
  4. Plan a week (NP5). The plate editor is one day in a week of breakfast, lunch, snacks and dinner. Pick a day to edit it. Week totals are compared with seven times the daily target. Copy this day onto another day, or onto Monday–Friday; you will be asked to confirm before those days are replaced. The week starts on Monday unless you change the start day; that choice stays on this device. A month planner is not in this release.
  5. Sex and targets. Targets follow ICMR-NIN style figures and stay approximate. Iron is higher for most females from the pre-teen years to adulthood. Prefer not to say uses the mid-point of the female and male figures. Older adults use the same iron figure for both.
  6. Games. Guess the calories of a food, fix an unbalanced plate by adding and removing foods, or pick a healthier swap. Best scores stay on this device. Games always use the default household portions, not the sizes you set on the Portions tab.
  7. Save. The current week, named plates and named days are stored in this browser only. Each save stores the grams actually used, so changing a portion later does not rewrite old days or other days in the week.
  8. More foods (NP2 and NP3). A Leafy greens group (lai xaak, khutora, sojina paat, kolmou, morapat, kochu xaak, dhekia and more) and a Condiments group (ginger, garlic, chillies, spices, nemu, tamarind, outenga, pickle, kharisa and mustard oil). Vegetables now include tomato, onion, carrot, gourds such as potol and komora, tubers such as kochu and ol, and garden foods such as koldil, posola and sojina. Grains add Basmati, Joha, bora saul and red rice. Meat and fish add pork, dry fish (hukoti, namsing / sidol), plain broiler and masor tenga. Search works with local names. Assam foods with no exact table row use the closest IFCT 2017 food, so they stay approximate. Leafy greens count as vegetables on the plate; condiments sit on the side.
