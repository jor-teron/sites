title: Choking (Adult)
icon: 😮
summary: Cough, 5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts.
call: Call emergency services right away if the person cannot breathe, cough or speak, or if they become unresponsive. Ask someone else to call while you help.

[basics]
1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough, speak or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing. Do not slap their back yet.
2. If they cannot cough, speak or breathe: lean them forward and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. | drawing: choking-back-blows
3. Still choking? Give up to 5 abdominal thrusts: stand behind them, put a fist just above the belly button, hold it with your other hand and pull sharply inwards and upwards. | drawing: choking-thrusts
4. Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or help arrives.
5. If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, make sure emergency services are called and start CPR.

[expert]
- Mild choking: the person can cough, speak or cry. A strong cough is the best way to clear it, so encourage it. Severe choking: they cannot speak or breathe, may clutch the throat, go silent or turn blue.
- Late pregnancy or a very large person: give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.
- Anyone who has needed abdominal thrusts should be checked by a doctor afterwards, because the thrusts can cause internal injury.
- Do not sweep the mouth blindly with a finger. Only remove an object you can see and easily reach.
- Babies under 1 year need a different technique (5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts, never abdominal thrusts). This card is for adults; take a first aid course to learn it.
