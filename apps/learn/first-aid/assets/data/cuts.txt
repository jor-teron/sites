title: Cuts and Scrapes
icon: 🩹
summary: Stop the bleeding, clean it, cover it.
call: Call emergency services if the bleeding is heavy, spurts, or does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure.

[basics]
1. Wash your hands with soap and water, or put on clean gloves if you have them.
2. Press a clean cloth or gauze firmly on the cut. Keep pressing for 10 minutes without lifting it to check. | drawing: cuts-pressure
3. If blood soaks through, put more cloth on top and keep pressing. Do not remove the first layer.
4. When the bleeding stops, rinse the cut gently under clean running water and wash the skin around it with mild soap.
5. Pat dry and cover with a clean dressing or bandage. Change it daily, or sooner if it gets wet or dirty.

[expert]
- Why pressure works: firm pressure squeezes the damaged blood vessels shut so a clot can form. Lifting early to look breaks the clot.
- Raising an injured arm or leg above the heart can slow the bleeding. Do not move a limb you think may be broken.
- Do not use a tourniquet unless bleeding from a limb is life threatening and firm pressure cannot control it. Follow the emergency dispatcher's instructions.
- See a doctor for deep or gaping cuts, cuts over joints, animal or human bites, wounds from dirty or rusty objects, or if you are unsure when you last had a tetanus vaccination.
- Signs of infection: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, or fever. See a doctor if you notice any of them.
- Avoid putting cotton wool directly on a wound, because the fibres stick to it.
