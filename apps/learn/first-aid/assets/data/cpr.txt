title: CPR (Adult)
icon: ❤️
summary: Call, push hard and fast, use an AED.
call: Call emergency services first, or ask someone to, and ask for an AED (defibrillator). Do not delay starting compressions.

[basics]
1. Check the scene is safe. Tap their shoulders and shout. If they do not respond and are not breathing normally (not breathing, or only gasping), act now.
2. Call emergency services, or send someone, and ask for an AED. Put the phone on speaker so you can follow the dispatcher.
3. Lay the person on their back on a firm surface and kneel beside them.
4. Put the heel of one hand on the centre of the chest, the other hand on top, arms straight. Push hard and fast: about 5 cm (2 inches) deep, 100 to 120 times a minute. Let the chest come back up fully each time. | drawing: cpr-hands
5. After 30 compressions, give 2 rescue breaths if you are trained: tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose and breathe in for about 1 second, watching the chest rise. If you are not trained or not willing, keep doing compressions only.
6. Keep going with 30 compressions then 2 breaths. When the AED arrives, switch it on and follow its voice instructions. Do not stop until emergency responders take over, the person starts breathing normally, or you cannot carry on.

[expert]
- Depth and rate: about 5 to 6 cm (2 to 2.4 inches) at 100 to 120 a minute. Do not lean on the chest between pushes. If someone else can help, swap about every 2 minutes.
- Keep pauses as short as possible, ideally under 10 seconds.
- An AED is safe for anyone to use. It checks the heart rhythm and only gives a shock if one is needed. Stand clear while it analyses and shocks. Dry a wet chest first and follow the pictures on the pads.
- Hands-only CPR (compressions with no breaths) is a good option for an untrained bystander helping an adult.
- Gasping (agonal breathing) is not normal breathing. Start CPR.
- Children and babies need a changed technique. Learn it in a certified first aid course.
