title: Burns and Scalds
icon: 🔥
summary: Cool it with running water for 20 minutes.
call: Call emergency services for burns that are large, deep, on the face, hands, feet, genitals or over a joint, for chemical or electrical burns, or if the person has trouble breathing.

[basics]
1. Move away from the heat. If electricity is involved, switch it off at the source before you touch the person.
2. Cool the burn under cool (not ice cold) running water for 20 minutes, as soon as possible.
3. Gently take off rings, watches and tight clothes near the burn before it swells. Do not pull off anything stuck to the skin.
4. After cooling, cover the burn loosely with cling film laid lengthways (do not wrap it round), or a clean non-fluffy dressing.
5. Keep the rest of the person warm. Pain relief from the pharmacy may help; follow the label.

[expert]
- Do not use ice, iced water, butter, oil, toothpaste or creams on a fresh burn. Ice can damage the skin further and greasy products trap heat.
- Do not burst blisters. They protect the skin underneath.
- Depth: a red, painful burn is shallow. Blistering, or white, brown or charred skin (which may not hurt) means a deeper burn that needs medical care.
- Scalds from hot liquid: take off soaked clothing unless it is stuck to the skin, then cool in the same way.
- Any burn bigger than the person's hand, and any burn in a baby or young child, should be seen by a medical professional.
- Chemical burns: brush off dry powder first, then flush with plenty of water and keep flushing until help arrives.
