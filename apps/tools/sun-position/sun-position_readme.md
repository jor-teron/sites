# Sun Position (SP3)

Offline sun-position app. No libraries, no network. It opens on Diphu, then you can use this device's location, pick a city, or type coordinates.

- Files: `sun-position.html`, `sun-position.css`, `sun-position.js` (RELEASE = 'SP3', `?v=SP3`), `sp_calc.js` (NOAA solar maths and Meeus moon, node-testable), `sp_wmm.js` (WMM2025 declination, copied from the Lab Compass), `sp_cities.js` (Indian state and UT capitals plus major world cities, each with an IANA time zone), `index.html` redirect, `sun-position_icon.svg`, `test_sp_calc.js` (`node test_sp_calc.js`).
- Storage: localStorage `sun-position-v1` = `{theme:'auto'|'light'|'dark', tzMode:'ist'|'place', places:[{id,name,lat,lon,tz}], currentPlace, askedPlace, shadowH, alerts}`. Auto theme follows `sites-hub.theme` (else the device setting). Saved places and the current place stay on the device. Add or remove them with in-app dialogs (no `alert` / `confirm`).
- Location: `navigator.geolocation` (used only when you ask), the built-in city list, or typed latitude and longitude. GPS and custom places store a time zone (the device zone, IST, or one you pick).
- Times: IST is the default. “This place” uses the city's IANA zone, or the zone saved with a custom / GPS place. A date picker and a time slider (shared by both tabs) show the sun at any hour; Now returns to a live view (refreshed every 15 seconds, not a render loop).
- Now tab: a compass with N at the top, today's above-horizon path as an arc, and the sun at its azimuth and height (centre is straight up, the rim is the horizon). A dashed arrow shows the shadow direction when the sun is up. Direction is degrees clockwise from north. Height includes NOAA refraction. Times list: sunrise, sunset, solar noon, day length, first and last light (civil twilight), golden hour (elevation −4° to 6°) and blue hour (−6° to −4°). Polar day and night are named plainly when the sun does not set or rise.
- Plan tab:
  - Shadow: type a height in metres to get the length and direction at the chosen date and time (same units, also drawn on the dial).
  - Year: an SVG chart of sunrise and sunset (daylight band) through the year, with solstices, equinoxes, and the longest and shortest days labelled.
  - Sun path: a polar diagram of the sun's track on the 21st of each month, with clock-hour lines, plus a best year-round fixed solar-panel tilt and azimuth (clear-sky geometry only, no weather).
  - Save picture: an in-app dialog saves the dial, year chart or sun path as a PNG on this device.
- Maths: NOAA Solar Calculator (Meeus) in `sp_calc.js`. Official sunrise and sunset use a 90.833° zenith. Checked against known values for Delhi, Guwahati, London and Tromsø, plus the Golden, Colorado 2003-10-17 NOAA/NREL example, year marks, and panel facing (Delhi south, Sydney north).
- Point tab: hold the phone up (as if taking a photo of the sky). After you grant motion permission on iOS, a heading-up compass shows the sun, the moon, and sunrise/sunset directions. An arrow and short text tell you to turn left or right and to tilt up or down. Magnetic heading is corrected to true north with the offline WMM2025 model at the current place. On a device with no compass, the same marks are shown with north at the top.
- Moon: azimuth and height, phase name and percent lit, moonrise and moonset for the current place and date (Meeus, tested against the 1992-04-12 worked example and known new/full moons).
- Alerts: optional, only while the app is open. 15 minutes before sunrise, sunset or golden hour, a banner appears; if you allow the Notification API, a system notification is sent too. Turning alerts on may ask for that permission. Denied permission still keeps the in-app banner.
- Limits: times are usually within one or two minutes of an almanac; the NOAA method is less accurate above about 72° latitude. Refraction is an average, not adjusted for temperature or pressure. The city list is a small offline table, not a complete gazetteer. A custom place does not guess its time zone from the map; you pick one. The panel tilt is a geometric estimate, not a forecast of energy with cloud cover. PNG save needs a browser that can draw an SVG onto a canvas (most current ones). Pointing needs a phone compass (and motion permission on iOS) and works best through the Hub on https; tilt uses the back of the phone, so hold it as if taking a photo. Moon rise/set times are usually within about ten minutes of an almanac. Alerts do not fire when the tab is in the background. Needs the Hub menu refresh from Main Builder after this is pushed.
